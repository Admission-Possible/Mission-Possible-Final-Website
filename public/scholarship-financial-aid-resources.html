Scholarship & Financial-Aid Resources

Understand the cost. Explore the help.

Start with official aid information and each college’s financial-aid office. Eligibility and requirements depend on your situation, school and application year.

Explore this guide ↓

Understand an aid offer.

01

Grants and scholarships

These generally do not require repayment, but conditions can apply. Check eligibility, renewal requirements and what happens if enrollment changes.

02

Work-study

An opportunity to earn money through eligible work. An award is not the same as cash available upfront to pay a bill.

03

Loans

Borrowed money that must be repaid under the loan’s terms. Compare the amount, interest and repayment obligations before accepting.

Approach the FAFSA with a plan.

  1. Use the official starting point

    Go to StudentAid.gov for the correct FAFSA form, current instructions and eligibility information. Do not rely on a deadline from a previous year.

  2. Check all deadlines

    Federal, state and college deadlines may differ. Colleges may also require additional aid forms.

  3. Follow the contributor instructions

    Use official guidance to understand who needs to provide information. Each person should keep their own account credentials private.

  4. Review and follow up

    Check the processed application and respond to requests from colleges. Contact the aid office if your circumstances need explanation.

Compare net costs, not just awards.

List tuition, housing, meals, fees, books and travel. Subtract grants and scholarships to estimate net cost; consider loans and work separately. Ask what may change in later years and whether an award renews.
